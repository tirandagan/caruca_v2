Tiran Dagan (the user) is a PhD student leading `caruca_v2`. He has two PhD advisors, only one of whom
is connected to Caruca:

- **Prof. Michael Greenberg** (mgreenbe@stevens.edu) — PhD advisor AND the PI/advisor for Caruca itself
  (he's also a co-author on the Caruca white paper, see [[caruca-v2-project-overview]]). Primary point
  of contact for anything Caruca-specific.
- **Prof. William Eiers** (weiers@stevens.edu) — a second PhD advisor to Tiran, but has **no involvement
  in Caruca**. Don't assume he needs to be looped in on Caruca-specific decisions or correspondence.
  His own research background is security/formal-methods, not shell-spec-mining: PhD "Quantitative Access
  Control Policy Analysis and Repair Using Model Counting" (UC Santa Barbara, Sept 2023, advised by Tevfik
  Bultan) — model counting for string/numeric constraints, the ABC2 constraint solver, and **Quacky**, an
  open-source tool for quantifying permissiveness of AWS IAM / Azure access-control policies, plus
  automated policy repair for overly-permissive policies (ICSE/ASE/ISSTA/FSE publications). Full text is
  in this repo: `ai_docs/refs/eiers_dissertation.md` (converted 2026-08-29, all 29 figures/23 tables/24
  algorithm listings extracted to `ai_docs/refs/images/eiers-*.png`; source PDF alongside it at
  `ai_docs/refs/eiers_dissertation.pdf`, originally from
  `/mnt/d/Dropbox/03 Academic/Stevens/06 Readings/William Eiers/Quantitative_Access_Control_Po.pdf` on the
  Dropbox D: drive). This explains why his Aug 2026 guidance on caruca_v2 scope (see
  [[caruca-v2-baseline-scope]]) reads as an outside-domain steer toward rigor/scoping rather than
  shell-spec-mining expertise.

**Why this matters:** don't conflate "Tiran's advisor" with "Caruca stakeholder" — only Greenberg is both.
CLAUDE.md in `~/stevens/caruca_v2` reflects this distinction.
